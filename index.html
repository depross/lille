More about the marathon

Lille hasn't had a marathon since 1995. This is the comeback, 31 years later, and the city went slightly mad for it: 18,000 bibs sold out in under two hours on the evening registration opened.

Three races share the day. The half-marathon goes off at 08:30, the marathon at 11:15 from boulevard de la Liberté, beside parc Jean-Baptiste Lebas. The first kilometres run past Grand Palais, round Lille Flandres, up Rue Faidherbe across Grand'Place and out through Vieux-Lille; the last ones come back along the boulevard, under the hotel's windows, to finish on the Esplanade du Champ de Mars by the Citadelle. Switch on the course in the map legend to see it.

For us it means three things. The course runs up Rue Pierre Mauroy past the hotel door and across Grand'Place, so expect barriers and a wait at the crossings between roughly 11:00 and 14:00. It also loops around the Grand Palais block, which is one more reason the Buzz plan needs a written confirmation. And the road closures run to 20:00. On foot, none of it is a problem. By car, we follow the signs and leave from a car park that is confirmed open.

Leave the bags with reception; the room comes later.

224 Rue Pierre Mauroy, 59000 LilleOpen in MapsCall

More

The building is older than the city's belfry. A wealthy merchant, Jean de la Cambe, known as Gantois, founded it in 1462 as a hospice for the poor, and it kept that role for more than five centuries, until 1995.

The Flemish façade on Rue Pierre Mauroy is from the 15th century, the chapel still stands, and the four inner courtyards are what the hotel was built around when it opened in 2003. The spa, the massage and breakfast are all inside the old walls.

Historic Lille

Grand'Place and its Flemish façades, the Vieille Bourse next to it, then Place Rihour and the streets around.

Leisurely. Not everything. Grand'Place is 12 minutes from the hotel.

Grand'PlaceVieille BoursePlace Rihour

More

The column in the middle of Grand'Place is the Déesse, raised in 1845 to remember the siege of 1792, when the city held out against the Austrians. The ornate 1930s building with the lettering on the roof is the old Voix du Nord newspaper.

The Vieille Bourse is twenty-four identical merchants' houses around one courtyard, built in 1652 just before Lille became French. The courtyard is the best bit: second-hand booksellers and chess players most afternoons.

Place Rihour has the remains of the Dukes of Burgundy's palace, now the tourist office. The streets between it and Grand'Place are the shopping ones.

Cobbles, boutiques, Méert

Rue Esquermoise and the streets around it. Méert for the vanilla waffles.

Méert, 27 Rue EsquermoiseOpen in Maps

More

Méert has been on this street since 1761, with the shop interior from 1839 barely touched. The waffle is thin, oval and filled with Madagascar vanilla cream, made to the same recipe since 1849. De Gaulle, born in Lille, had them sent to Paris.

There is a tearoom at the back if it's cold, and a queue at the counter if it's not. Rue de la Monnaie and Place aux Oignons, just behind, are the prettiest cobbles in the old town.

Dinner at Eva Restaurant

Franco-Russian inspired. Reservation at 19:00, fixed.

4 Rue Saint-Étienne, 59800 LilleOpen in MapsCall

More

Eva took over the old Compostelle, a Renaissance townhouse on Rue Saint-Étienne with a glass roof over the main room. The house has been a pilgrims' inn and, during the Occupation, something less respectable.

The kitchen does contemporary Eastern European cooking, Russian-leaning: salmon koulibiac, pâté en croûte, caramelised blinis, a rose pavlova. Early evening is calm and dressed up; the music rises later on. We'll be in the calm part.

13:30 – 15:00

Palais des Beaux-Arts

Open Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, €7 each. One of the largest collections in France: Flemish and Dutch masters, Rubens, Goya, Rodin. It sits on Place de la République, right beside the marathon start, so expect crowds around it and go in from the square. Seven minutes from the hotel.

Smaller option: Musée de l'Hospice Comtesse in Vieux-Lille. Check its Sunday hours on the day.

Place de la République, 59000 LilleOpen in Maps

More

Opened in 1892 in a palace built for the purpose, and still counted as the second museum of France after the Louvre. Rubens and Van Dyck upstairs, Goya's two famous old women, Delacroix, Courbet, and the Impressionists in a smaller room.

The basement has something unexpected: the relief plans, enormous 18th-century scale models of the fortified towns of the north, Lille included. Ninety minutes is enough if we pick two floors and don't try to do everything.
